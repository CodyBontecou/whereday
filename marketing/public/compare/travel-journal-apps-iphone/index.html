Travel journal apps for iPhone, compared

Choose a travel journal by the questions that matter: does it record for you, where does it live, can you export it, and what does it cost. Whereday publishes this page and is one of the apps compared.

By Cody Bontecou, Whereday’s developer · Reviewed November 24, 2026

Short answer: pick the kind of travel journal first

Disclosure: Whereday publishes this page and is one of the apps compared. Every app is judged on the same four questions, and each claim about another product links to that vendor’s own page, help article or App Store listing, checked on November 24, 2026.

If you want a social, shareable trip blog with photos and a printed book, look at the Polarsteps, FindPenguins and Journi group. If you want to write, with location as context, look at Day One or Apple Journal. If you want an automatic, private record of every place and route that you can export, look at Whereday. Many people use one app from each group; see how to combine them.

The four questions that sort these apps

  1. Does it record where I went on its own, or do I type every entry? Some apps track your route automatically, some make you write posts, and some combine the two. It decides how much you have to do while travelling.
  2. Where does my journal live? On the device, or in an account on the vendor’s cloud, and whether it is public or social by default. This decides who can see your trips and whether you need an account.
  3. Can I get it out? Which files you can export (GPX, JSON, CSV, Markdown, PDF or a printed book). A trip record is only useful years later if you can still open it, including after you stop paying.
  4. What does it cost? Prices change and differ by country, so we link each vendor’s own pricing page or listing instead of copying every number.

Where we found no statement by the vendor, the cell says “Not stated by the vendor”. That means we found nothing in the sources linked, not that the feature is absent.

Polarsteps

Does well: the App Store listing promises “Automatic route tracking” on a world map, trip updates friends and family can follow, and a printed Travel Book of your route and memories. Privacy controls let you decide who sees a trip: “everyone, followers only, or you only”. The help centre describes a “Download my data” request in your account settings.

Doesn’t do (per its help centre): Polarsteps says you cannot export a trip to a PDF file right now (help) and cannot import a GPX file (help). We found no GPX export statement.

Sources: App Store listing, export a copy of my data, PDF export.

FindPenguins

Does well: its listing says to “Activate our Travel Tracker to automatically record your travel route – battery-friendly and offline, with your phone in your pocket”, and to turn memories into “a carefully-designed hardcover travel book”. It offers sharing live location with a time delay. Its help centre explains how to export a GPX file of a trip route and download a PDF eBook, and the GPX help also describes importing a route into an empty trip.

Doesn’t do: we found no statement on whether you need an account, or on a full data export beyond the GPX and PDF options above. Those export steps are described for the website rather than the app.

Sources: App Store listing, GPX export, PDF eBook.

Journi

This is Journi Blog from Journi GmbH, not the unrelated health-benefits service at journi.com.

Does well: the listing says you can “Use Journi offline, without Internet”, choose who sees each story (“Just for you, Secret, Only on request or Public”), back up journals to Dropbox and Google Drive, save photos “in full size and high resolution”, and order a photo book. It says Journi groups photos “smartly by time and location data”.

Doesn’t do (or doesn’t say): the listing does not describe automatic route tracking, whether you need an account, or a journal export file format. Its in-app purchase is Journi Premium Membership, with several prices on the listing; we don’t copy them because they vary.

Sources: App Store listing; the company’s photo book site.

Day One

Does well: Day One is a writing app. Its features page says “Time, date, weather, step count, and other key data is magically added to every entry”, offers a map view of past entries, and promises “end-to-end encryption”. Its pricing page lists a free Basic plan with unlimited journals, PDF and CSV/JSON export on all tiers, and paid Silver ($49.99 per year) and Gold ($74.99 per year) plans. Free covers one device and one photo per entry; paid plans cover unlimited devices and 30 photos or videos per entry.

Doesn’t do (or doesn’t say): we found no statement that it records a route or detects visits on its own. Its listing says it “may use your location even when it isn’t open”, but describes no route recording. A GPX export is not stated by the vendor.

Sources: features, pricing, App Store listing.

Apple Journal

Does well: Apple’s guide says Journaling Suggestions group outings, photos, workouts and more, and that this data is private and stored on your iPhone. You can lock the journal with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode, sync with iCloud, or include entries in an encrypted local backup. You can print entries or export them as PDFs.

Doesn’t do (or doesn’t say): the pages checked describe no continuous route recording, and no export other than print and PDF. Price is not stated on them.

Sources: the three Apple iPhone User Guide pages linked above.

Whereday

We make Whereday, so weigh this section accordingly. Does well: it detects visits automatically and records continuous GPS routes (tracking docs), has an Apple Watch app that records GPS on its own (Watch docs), lets you add manual visits and notes, and counts Country Days (guide). The working history is stored on your iPhone and there is no Whereday account (privacy and data). It exports JSON, CSV, Markdown and GPX, among other formats (export docs).

Doesn’t do: it is not a social trip blog, it has no public sharing page and no printed book, and it has no iCloud sync (its export and import files are the backup path). It is built to record, not to write long stories.

Access: from version 1.8, new users need an eligible trial, subscription or Lifetime purchase to track. Existing users keep free tracking and prior purchase rights. New users keep file export after a subscription ends; existing free users keep their earlier export-unlock requirement. See the homepage.

Sources: the Whereday documentation.

Combine a recorder and a journal

A practical pattern: let Whereday record the places and routes automatically, then write the story in your journal app of choice. After a trip, export a day of visits as Markdown and paste it into your journal, or follow the Markdown and Obsidian guide if you want your journal as plain files. For a route to show on a map, see how to record a route as GPX.

Count days in each country with Country Days, or plot the places on the visited countries map. For trips from before you installed anything, see how to find your travel history. If you are comparing location logs rather than journals, read Location history apps for iPhone.

Keep a copy that will still open later

Whichever app you use, export before a trial or subscription ends and keep the file somewhere you control. Pick the format by purpose: GPX for a route you want on a map, CSV or JSON for data, Markdown or PDF to read; the GPX, KML or GeoJSON guide helps with the choice. Then follow the backup checklist and test that the file opens.

FAQ

Is there a travel journal app that works offline?

FindPenguins’ listing says its Travel Tracker records your route “offline”, and Journi’s says “Use Journi offline, without Internet”. Whereday stores its history on your iPhone, and its Apple Watch app records GPS on its own. Check each app’s own page for details.

Which travel journal apps export GPX?

FindPenguins documents a GPX route export from its website. Whereday exports GPX. For Polarsteps, Journi, Day One and Apple Journal, a GPX export is not stated by the vendor in the sources we checked.

Can I make a travel journal without posting it publicly?

Yes, in several. Polarsteps offers “you only”, Journi offers “Just for you”, and Apple Journal can be locked with Face ID, Touch ID or a passcode. Day One says entries are end-to-end encrypted, and Whereday has no public sharing and no account.

Do these apps record my route automatically?

Polarsteps, FindPenguins and Whereday say they do. Day One and Apple Journal are writing apps, and Journi’s listing does not describe route tracking.
