    How the 90/180 rule works

    Short stays in the Schengen area are limited to 90 days in any 180-day period (Regulation (EU) 2016/399, Article 6). The 180 days are a rolling window, not a calendar half-year. On any day you check, look back over the 180 days that end on that day, including it.

    • Entry and exit days both count. A trip from 1 to 3 January is 3 days.
    • A date in two Schengen states counts once. Overlapping trips do too.
    • Every day of a stay has its own window. That is why the planner checks each day, not only the last one.
    • Days drop out. The date a day stops counting is 180 days after it, so your allowance returns gradually and a stay can be longer than “90 minus days used”.

    The European Commission’s short-stay calculator is the official reference for the same count. To count days in every country, or 183-day totals, use the days-in-country calculator.

    What doesn’t count towards the 90 days

    Stays covered by a national long-stay visa or a residence permit are outside the short-stay count, and some nationalities have bilateral agreements with individual Schengen states. This calculator does not model either, so leave those stays out. Check with the state concerned, and see the European Commission’s short-stay calculator page and Schengen area page.

    Official records win

    At the Schengen external border, the Entry/Exit System records entries and exits of non-EU short-stay travellers and has replaced passport stamping since 10 April 2026. Where your record and an official record differ, the official record is what counts. Need past dates? See how to find your travel history from official and personal records.

    Limits of this calculator

    • Not legal advice. It does not decide your visa or status.
    • It counts dates you enter. It does not know your visa type, and missing trips make the count too low.
    • Overstays are judged by border authorities, not by this page.
    • Planned stays check their own days. Trips you have already entered after the planned dates are counted, but are not re-checked.
    • Not modelled: microstates and special territories, long-stay visas and residence permits, bilateral agreements, and member-state join dates.
