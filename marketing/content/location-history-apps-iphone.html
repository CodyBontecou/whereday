Short answer: pick by the job you need done

Disclosure: Whereday publishes this page and is one of the apps compared. Every app below is judged on the same criteria, and each claim about another product links to its own documentation or App Store listing.

If you want a private journal with portable files and no account, look at Whereday or Arc Timeline 4. If you already pay for Arc, check its current terms before you rely on it. If you want your own server, send OwnTracks or Overland points to Dawarich. If you want to see family members, that is a different job, so use Life360 or Find My. No app can rebuild days that were never recorded.

Built-in Apple features

Apple’s Location Services & Privacy page says Significant Locations keeps “places you have recently been” and “the routes you take” to give you personalized features, such as predictive routes and Memories in Photos. Apple says this is end-to-end encrypted and cannot be read by Apple. Apple documents no export, so you cannot take that list out as a file. Read Significant Locations and Visited Places explained for what each keeps.

Find My shows the current location, or the last known location for up to seven days, according to Apple’s Find My article. Apple documents no history view. See whether Find My keeps location history.

Whereday

We make Whereday, so weigh this section accordingly. It records visits and GPS routes on your iPhone. The Apple Watch app can record GPS on its own and queues the transfer to your iPhone (Apple Watch docs). Records are stored locally and there is no Whereday account (privacy and data).

Export writes eight formats: JSON, CSV, Markdown, GPX, KML, GeoJSON, OwnTracks and Overland (export and import docs). Import and Export Preview are free. Whereday imports Google Timeline JSON in the raw, legacy semantic and on-device formats, and its own JSON, CSV, Markdown and backup files. It does not import GPX, KML or GeoJSON.

Access rules. From version 1.8, new users need an eligible trial, subscription, or Lifetime purchase to track. Existing users keep free tracking and prior purchase rights. New users keep file export after a subscription ends. Existing free users keep their earlier export-unlock requirement.

Arc Timeline 4

This is Arc Timeline, the location app from Big Paua, not the Arc browser. The current app is Arc Timeline 4. Its listing says “All data stored on your device”, “No accounts, no sign-ups” and “Export your data anytime in standard JSON format”. It also says: “A free trial is included. An active subscription or lifetime purchase is required after the trial period.” Big Paua’s Arc page says data is stored on iPhone, with no account and optional iCloud backup, and that you can “export your data at any time, as JSON or GPX”.

The original Arc Timeline listing (id1063151918) says in its 3.17.4 release notes: “All new subscriptions and Lifetime purchases now happen in Arc Timeline 4. This app stays in place for existing subscribers.”

On what happens when you stop paying, Big Paua’s developer wrote on its support forum (November 2025, before Arc Timeline 4): “Currently you need an active subscription to view your data in the app. You can however export your data to GPX and then view it in other apps, but that’s not really ideal.” We found no Arc Timeline 4 statement on this, so we don’t claim it applies. The safe advice is to export while your access is active.

Google Maps Timeline

Per Google’s Timeline help for iPhone, visits and routes are saved on signed-in devices. Timeline is off by default and opt-in, and you need a Google account. Backup is an encrypted copy on Google’s servers and requires auto-delete to be off. Auto-delete runs at 3, 18 or 36 months. To export, open your profile picture → Settings → Location & Privacy → Export Timeline data, which saves location-history.json. For depth, read Whereday and Google Timeline compared.

Self-hosted: OwnTracks or Overland to Dawarich

OwnTracks sends to “a server of your choice” (OwnTracks booklet). Dawarich is a server you run, and its tracking docs list Overland, OwnTracks, Traccar Client and GPSLogger, and name official Dawarich apps for iOS and Android. It imports Google Timeline JSON, and its pricing page says “Self-host for free — every Pro feature, your server, zero cost”, with paid hosted plans.

The trade-off is effort: you run, secure and back up the server yourself. Whereday can also send points to Dawarich; see Dawarich integration.

Family sharing apps

Life360 and Find My do a different job: they share live location with people you choose, and aren’t built as a personal journal. The Life360 listing describes family safety and location sharing. If that is what you need, use them. Read does Find My keep location history for what Apple keeps.

Questions to ask before you commit

  • Can I export before the trial ends? Do it before then.
  • Does a GPX or CSV export open in another tool on day one? Test it with a short outing (see the GPX viewer and export docs).
  • Are the battery and permission settings right? Read location tracking and battery.
  • Do I keep the original file after any export or import? Keep it, and see backing up your history.
  • What can I see if I stop paying? Check the cell for that app above.

FAQ

Does iPhone keep a location history?

Apple keeps Significant Locations, which it says is encrypted and unreadable by Apple, with no documented export. See Significant Locations.

Is there an app that tracks everywhere I go?

Several apps record visits or routes; the table shows how each does it. Any app is limited by permissions, battery settings and the days it was running.

Can I export my location history from iPhone?

Not from Apple’s built-in features, which document no export. Apps like Whereday, Arc Timeline 4 and Google Maps Timeline offer exports. See how to see your location history.
