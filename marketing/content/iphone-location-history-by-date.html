Quick answer: where was I on a specific date?

  • Apple Maps: open Maps → Places → Visited Places and filter by date. Apple documents this for iOS 26 and later, where available, and it only has visits if the feature was on and Keep Visits hadn’t removed them.
  • Google Maps: if you used Timeline, tap your profile picture, then Your Timeline, and select the date.
  • If neither was on, rebuild the day from records that carry a real date: photos, workouts, calendar events, receipts and messages. They show where you were probably, not a route.

Don’t use Significant Locations & Routes for this: Apple documents no date filter for it. Find My keeps no history at all.

Apple sources: Visited Places (iOS 26), Clear location history in Maps and Location Services & Privacy. Details of the first two features are in the Significant Locations and Visited Places guide.

Apple Maps: jump to a date in Visited Places

  1. Open Maps and tap Places, then Visited Places.
  2. Use the filter to narrow the list by date, category or city. You can also search by name, category, address or notes, or tap All Visits to see every visit.
  3. Scroll to the bottom and check Keep Visits. It sets how long Maps keeps visits, so a date older than that choice won’t be there.

Apple’s guide doesn’t spell out the filter controls, so labels on your screen may differ. It documents no export for Visited Places. To keep what you find, a screenshot is the only way; it’s a picture, not data you can import anywhere. Whereday can’t read or recover Visited Places.

If Visited Places is missing from Maps on your iPhone, see how to turn it on and what it needs. Turning it on now saves visits from now on, not earlier dates.

Google Maps Timeline: pick the date

  1. Open Google Maps, tap your profile picture or initial, then Your Timeline.
  2. Select the date you want.

Timeline is stored on the device, and the auto-delete setting you chose applies, so older dates may be gone. If Timeline was off that day, there is nothing to show. Google changes these screens during rollouts; if yours differ, follow Google Maps Help: Manage your Timeline. For backups, moving to a new phone and export, read the Google Maps Timeline guide. If you saved a Google JSON file, you can import it into Whereday with a preview.

Rebuild a day from records you already have

When no location history exists for the date, work through these. Each has a real date attached.

  1. Photos, by day. In Photos, tap Collections, scroll to Recent Days and tap a day. Apple’s guide also covers Years and Months views, where All shows every photo and video (Find photos and videos by date).
  2. Photo location and time. Open a photo and swipe up or tap the Info button. Apple says the details show when the photo was taken and where; tap the location link to open it in Maps (See photo and video information). For all photos on a map, see how to see your photos on a map.
  3. Workouts. If you recorded a run, walk or ride with Fitness or an Apple Watch, open that workout from the day. If it has a route map, it shows a path.
  4. Calendar. Open the day and read events, addresses and times. A calendar event is a plan; it doesn’t prove you went.
  5. Wallet and payment history. Card and Apple Pay transactions show a merchant, an amount and a time. They don’t show GPS coordinates.
  6. Messages and email. Search for receipts, bookings, ride confirmations and photos you sent, and check their timestamps.

Looking for whole trips rather than one date? See how to find your travel history.

Accuracy: a photo’s location is where the photo was taken, not where you spent the day. A transaction tells you a merchant and a time, not a coordinate; online payments may not be where you were at all. Treat the result as evidence for a timeline, and say so if you use it for a dispute or a claim. Photos in a time gap can also help place a stop you add later by hand.

Don’t change the Date & Time to “see” an old day

Some pages suggest turning off Set Automatically in Settings → General → Date & Time and choosing another date. We don’t recommend it. It only changes what time your iPhone believes it is. None of the Apple pages above describes setting the clock to reveal location history, and a record that was never saved doesn’t appear because the clock moved. A wrong clock can make sign-ins, certificates and sync fail, and time-based two-factor codes depend on an accurate clock (RFC 6238). If you’ve already changed it, turn Set Automatically back on.

Why the date is empty

  • The feature was off that day. Visited Places and Timeline only save from when they’re turned on.
  • Keep Visits removed it. Maps keeps visits only for the period you chose.
  • History was cleared. Clear History in Maps deletes visits; Timeline can be deleted by day, range or all.
  • New phone. Timeline lives on the device and doesn’t appear on a new one unless you restored a backup.
  • Auto-delete. Google’s auto-delete removes data past the period you set.

Common questions

Can I see where I was a year ago?

Only if something was recording then and still has it. Check Visited Places (within your Keep Visits choice), Google Timeline (within its auto-delete setting), photos and receipts.

Does Find My show past locations?

No. It shows a current or last known location only. See what Find My keeps.

Can I see the exact time I arrived?

Photo info shows the time a photo was taken, and a Wallet or calendar entry has a time. For an arrival time you need a recorded visit, such as one in Whereday.

Can I export one day?

Not from Apple’s Visited Places or Significant Locations; Apple documents no export. Whereday exports a chosen date range, including one day. Google Timeline has its own export in Settings.

Can I see history for a date before I installed an app?

No. An app can’t recover days it wasn’t recording, and it can’t read Apple’s or Google’s history. Use the sources above.

What about someone else’s phone?

This guide is for your own phone. If you’re worried about being tracked, see Apple’s Personal Safety guide.
