Add your files. They stay in this page.

Drop in a GPX, KML, GeoJSON, Google Timeline JSON or Whereday Country Days CSV file, or several. The countries your points fall in are shaded on the map and listed with the first date, last date and number of distinct dates, so you can check each one. No account, upload or analytics.

This is a record of where a phone was, not proof of where you lived or were present. GPS points can be wrong or missing, and this page is not legal or tax evidence. For day counts, use the days-in-country calculator.

      How points become countries

      1. Read. GPX track points, route points and waypoints; Google Timeline visits and timed points (accepted records only); GeoJSON Point, LineString and MultiLineString coordinates; and KML Point and LineString coordinates (not Google Earth’s gx:Track elements; convert such a KML to GPX first). A Country Days CSV already names its countries, so it needs no lookup. The format is detected from the file’s content, not its name.
      2. Thin. To keep long tracks fast, the page keeps one point per 0.01° cell (about 1 km) for each date. Every file’s list shows how many points were read and how many were kept.
      3. Look up. Each point is tested against country outlines stored in this page, using a bounding-box check and then a point-in-polygon test. Nothing is sent to a geocoder or map service. Only the points themselves are tested: the line between two points is not, so a flight is not a visit to the countries it crosses.
      4. Border and water. A point inside one country but within about 10 km of another country’s outline is counted as “near a border”, not as a visit. A point in no outline, such as open water, is “unmatched”. A country that only has such points is listed under “near a border or coast, not counted”, with a button to add it by hand.
      5. Dates. A timestamp with a UTC offset (such as +02:00) uses the date as written. A timestamp ending in Z uses its UTC date. Google Timeline times are read as UTC dates. Each file shows which rule it used. Points with no usable date still count towards the country, but add no date.

      The boundaries are Natural Earth 1:50m Admin 0 – Countries, version 5.1.2, which are in the public domain (terms of use). We simplified them to a tolerance of about 0.01° (roughly 1 km) and bundled them as a script, /assets/visited-countries-data.mjs: about 420 KB, about 150 KB gzipped, with 238 country and territory outlines (Antarctica included). The file formats are defined in the GPX 1.1 schema, GeoJSON (RFC 7946) and OGC KML 2.2.

      Longitudes are wrapped so that 180° and −180° are the same meridian, and the outlines are already split there, so a track that crosses the date line is handled. Points at the poles are tested like any other and usually come out unmatched, except in Antarctica.

      What counts as visited

      Here, a country counts if at least one of your points, after thinning, falls inside its outline and not within about 10 km of another country’s outline, or if you add it by hand. Time spent there does not matter, and neither does the reason for the stop. A layover counts only if your phone recorded a point there.

      • Natural Earth’s list. Its Admin 0 set includes many territories as their own entries, such as Greenland, Puerto Rico or Hong Kong, and includes Antarctica. Three entries without an ISO code (Northern Cyprus, Somaliland and Siachen Glacier) use Natural Earth’s own three-letter codes. Disputed areas follow Natural Earth’s choices, which not everyone agrees with. Dependencies it does not draw separately may show under another country or be missing.
      • Travel clubs. Clubs have their own lists. The Travelers’ Century Club, for example, counts a specific list of countries and territories, so your number there can differ from this one.
      • UN member states. The United Nations member list is shorter than any list that includes territories. This map does not filter to it.

      Limits

      • Boundaries are simplified. Outlines are at about 1:50,000,000 and cut down further for size. Points near borders and coastlines can land on the wrong side or in no country. That is why they are flagged rather than guessed. Small islands and very small states may be missing or crude.
      • Coastal cities. A point in a harbour or on a river mouth can fall just outside the coast outline and be unmatched. When it is within about 10 km of land the country is offered under “near a border or coast”.
      • Airports and layovers. Flying over a country is not a visit. Only recorded points are tested, and a phone in flight mode records nothing. A stop at an airport counts only if a point was recorded there.
      • Missing coverage. A file shows only what was recorded. Gaps, switched-off phones and trips before your file began do not appear. Add those by hand.
      • Trips before your file began. To rebuild them from official and personal records, see how to find your travel history.
      • Size. Each file can be up to 10 MiB and 50,000 points, the same limits as the GPX viewer. For more, split the export by date range.
      • Not a residence or tax record. GPS points show where a phone was, not where a person was. This page does not decide residency, visa days or tax status. For Schengen 90/180 and 183-day questions, use the days-in-country calculator, and check the official rules.

      Your file stays in this page

      Reading, matching, drawing the map and making the downloads all happen in your browser. This page blocks network connections and does not include the website’s analytics. The web server receives an ordinary request when you open the page and its scripts, but not your files, their names or the result. Clearing the page or closing the tab removes them; your original files are never changed.
